## TL;DR

1. **The simulator's headline is not a win probability against a human, and nothing in Week 4 tested it against one.**
   Round 1 shipped 66.6 ±2.4 % and lost the first human mock. Round 2 shipped 65.5 % from the same engine (plus one fix),
   without re-running the old six under the fixed engine and without a single human game on the new six.
2. **Trick Room games are not reproducible.** Replaying four Trick Room games of `hex2/engine2/nC19` from a fresh
   process: **3 of 4 diverged from the batch record and game 301 flipped from a win to a loss**; two non-TR replays
   matched exactly. Prime suspect: `trShare`'s memo (`ai/engine.mjs:481`) is keyed by slot ids only (`p1:0,p1:2|p2:1,…`),
   lives at module level, and is shared by both players and every game a worker plays, so the Trick Room term of the
   leaf value can read numbers computed for other sets. Same class as the slot-keyed damage-cache bug of Week 3. Not
   yet proven (§3.1).
3. **The closed-sheet belief is one modal set per species, and it is badly wrong for Hex's physical Garchomp.** Our
   engine believes every Garchomp is the Mega-Z set (Modest, 0 Atk, 52 % of pool weight). Against Life Orb Garchomp it
   thinks Rock Slide does 82–98 % to Charizard (real 110–132 %) and 45–55 % to Pelipper (real 63–74 %). Result: **0 of 21
   games won against Life Orb Garchomp, 5 of 19 against Scarf.** The "26 % vs physical Garchomp" weak spot is at least
   partly our own AI misreading the threat, so the sim can't tell us how bad the real matchup is.
4. **The weak spots were documented, not fixed.** Physical Garchomp (27 %), Garchomp + Kleavor leads (28–32 %), Forretress
   (44–46 %) and his Trick Room going up (56–57 %) are all still there in the shipped six. Two cheap set fixes were never
   tested: Ice Beam on Scarf Pelipper (Hydro Pump is redundant with Weather Ball) and Iron Fist Ice Punch Conkeldurr (the
   `xIF_ip` team is defined in `hex2conk.json` but was never run).
5. **Guts is the wrong Conkeldurr ability for a Bo3.** It ties Iron Fist only while Hex wrongly believes Iron Fist and
   burns Conkeldurr; once he knows (games 2–3), Guts is −4.3 ±5.8. Iron Fist does not depend on his mistake. Guts + Ice
   Punch (−16) is explained by calcs (below), not a mystery, and must never be used.
6. **Jacky's pool (gen_jacky.py) has silent item-clause damage:** Farigiraf holds Life Orb in 12 of his 16 weighted sixes
   (the builder moved its Sitrus Berry because Incineroar took it first), so our engine's belief of Farigiraf is a Life
   Orb set. The Jacky threat scan defends with base Kangaskhan, not Mega Kangaskhan, and the pool has no Close Combat
   Golisopod, though the scan itself shows Mega Golisopod Close Combat into Kangaskhan at 121–143 %.

## 1. What went well (keep doing)

- **Honest reporting.** Every report says when a result is a tie, a read, or a known loss (e.g. "no bring fixes it",
  "38–45 % whatever we led", "a read, not a rule"). Anthony's trust in the numbers depends on this. Keep it.
- **Paired seeds and confidence intervals.** Game i uses seed S + i in every run, the reports give paired intervals,
  and the `check-nC19` run (26 games) reproduced `engine2/nC19` game for game, so the pairing machinery works for
  non-Trick-Room games.
- **Building the opponent's best response** (round 1 §5, round 2 "Hex's side"). This found the h14 / Forretress counter
  shapes before the match. It is the right method; the failure was in what we concluded from it (§2.1 below).
- **Mechanics work for this matchup.** Psychic Terrain priority blocking, Expanding Force as a spread move, Cloud Nine,
  and (round 2) entry-hazard cost were added to the AI, each with tests. The hazard fix is a real fidelity gain.
- **Reacting quickly to Anthony's corrections.** The recoil re-check (Rocky Helmet and Rough Skin twice under Parental
  Bond, 33 % recoil) was thorough and changed three guide lines in the right direction.
- **Fast pivot after the mock.** Round 2 rebuilt the pool, screened 84 sixes, and re-ran the top two on fresh seeds
  within a day, and stayed inside Anthony's constraints (no Simipour / Stunfisk, Mega Kangaskhan main).
- **Psychic Spam was not abandoned.** Round 2's six still scores 66.9 % vs Indeedee-F + Armarouge (fresh seeds), so
  "don't over-correct" was respected in the sim's terms.

## 3. Simulator / engine fidelity

### 3.1 Determinism: Trick Room games don't replay (suspected memo leak)

`trShare` (`tools/sim/ai/engine.mjs:481–495`) memoises "how much Trick Room favours this side" by
`${m.key}${mega}` for each live mon, where `m.key` is `p1:0`, `p2:3`, … (slot ids, `ai/tracker.mjs:17`). No species,
no set. The `Map` is module-level, so within a worker it is shared by both players and by every game the worker plays,
and is cleared only past 5,000 entries. It feeds `P.trickRoom` in the value function whenever Trick Room is up
(`engine.mjs:450`, weight up to 0.6, about one Pokémon's worth).

Evidence (replays with `replayGame`, fresh process, same args file):

| Game (hex2/engine2/nC19) | TR up | Record | Fresh replay |
|---|---|---|---|
| 32 (xG_ip and xSF_ip runs) | no | loss 8 / win 5 turns | identical |
| 301 (h02) | yes | **win, 7 turns** | **loss, 9 turns** |
| 305 (h06) | yes | win, 8 turns | win, 7 turns |
| 317 (h18) | yes | win, 9 turns | win, 8 turns |
| 325 (h06) | yes | win, 8 turns | win, 8 turns |

A same-process check (301, then 305, then 301 again) gave the fresh-replay result both times, so the divergence needs
the specific history of the batch worker, not just any earlier game. What is established: **batch records of Trick
Room games do not reproduce standalone, non-TR games do.** What is suspected, not proven: `trShare`'s memo is the
leak (it is the only slot-keyed, module-level, TR-only state I found; the other caches, `dmgCache` and `statCache`,
are keyed by full set). Confirm by keying the memo by `setId` + side and replaying the same four games.

Consequences:
- Results depend on worker count and scheduling. `nC19-g2` (10 workers) changed **our own preview in 22 % of games**
  although only Hex's belief of us changed; nothing in our inputs changed, so our pick should not have moved at all.
- The viewer's promise "games replay exactly from their seed" is false for Trick Room games.
- Every paired comparison gets extra noise in the ~36 % of games where his Trick Room goes up, which is the plan the
  reports call his strongest. It may also bias them, since the stale value can have the wrong sign.
- Fix: key the memo by set (use `setId`) and side, or drop it; add a test that plays a TR game alone and after other
  games in the same worker and asserts identical logs. Then re-confirm headlines (rule 7).

### 3.2 Closed-sheet belief = one modal set, with leaks

`sheets.mjs library()` gives each species the single highest-weight set from the pool; `determinize`
(`engine/player.mjs:153`) then forces that set's spread, nature, ability and unrevealed moves onto the live Pokémon.

- **Wrong set for minority builds.** Garchomp's modal set is the Mega-Z set (52 %), so a Life Orb or Scarf Garchomp is
  searched as Modest 0 Atk with Draco / Earth Power / Power Gem in its unrevealed slots. App-engine calcs:

  | Life Orb Garchomp into | Real (Jolly 32 Atk) | What our engine believes |
  |---|---|---|
  | Mega Charizard Y, Rock Slide (sun) | 110–132 % | 82–98 % (thinks Charizard lives) |
  | Pelipper, Rock Slide (rain) | 63–74 % | 45–55 % |
  | Rotom-Fan, Rock Slide | 52–61 % | 38–47 % |
  | Morpeko, Earthquake | 190–225 % | 141–167 % |

  The 0/21 vs Life Orb Garchomp is the expected result of searching against the wrong numbers. Kleavor's modal share
  is 45 %, so Scarf / Life Orb Kleavor (h04, h13, h14, h18) is mis-modelled the same way: our engine believes Sash
  Kleavor at 150 Speed when Scarf Kleavor moves at 225.
- **Inconsistent item handling leaks the truth.** `determinize` replaces the item with the belief's only when neither
  is a Mega stone. Because Garchomp's belief item is a stone, the *real* Life Orb / Scarf is kept: the engine knows the
  item but not the spread. For other species the belief item overwrites the real one even after it has been revealed
  (e.g. Life Orb Kleavor's recoil is shown, but the engine keeps believing Focus Sash).
- **Our sets leak to the opponent.** Species missing from `beliefRef` fall back to `generic(real set)`, which keeps the
  real item, ability and moves. In hex2 the beliefRef is the Week 3 six (o01), so Hex's AI knew Scarf Pelipper's item
  and moves and Rotom-Fan's moves from turn 1 (pessimistic for us). In jacky.json, b01 lacks Sinistcha and Aegislash, so
  Jacky's AI will know Wide Guard Aegislash and our Sinistcha set from turn 1.
- **The belief is in-sample.** Our beliefs of Hex are built from the very pool the games are played against, so for
  his common sets "closed sheets" is close to open sheets for us. Against a real opponent whose sets differ, our engine
  will be more wrong than in the sim. The 65.5 % is optimistic on this axis.
- Fix: sample the belief set per game from the pool distribution (weighted), condition it on what has been revealed
  (item, moves, Mega or no Mega by turn 1), and include every species of our roster in `beliefRef`.

### 3.3 The −16 for Guts + Ice Punch is mostly explained

Paired vs `engine2/nC19`, recomputed:

| Variant | n | Diff | Same-pick games diff | Preview changed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| xIF_rs (Iron Fist, nC19 moves) | 376 | −0.3 ±5.3 | +3.4 (n 208) | 45 % |
| nC19-g2 (Hex believes Guts) | 305 | −4.3 ±5.8 | −6.3 (n 239) | 22 % |
| xSF_ip (Sheer Force, Ice Punch for Rock Slide) | 480 | −1.5 ±4.7 | −5.7 (n 192) | 60 % |
| **xG_ip (Guts, Ice Punch for Rock Slide)** | 480 | **−16.0 ±4.8** | −9.6 (n 249) | 48 % |
| xSF_rs (Sheer Force, no Mach Punch) | 480 | −15.4 ±5.6 | n/a | **100 %** (one lead in all 480) |

Calcs (Life Orb Brave Conkeldurr, app engine):

| Move into | Guts (no burn) | Sheer Force | Iron Fist |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ice Punch → Mega Garchomp Z | 80–96 % | **104–124 %** | 96–114 % |
| Ice Punch → Drampa | 80–96 % | 104–124 % | 96–114 % |
| Rock Slide → Rotom-Heat | 58–69 % | 76–91 % | 58–69 % |
| Drain Punch → Kleavor | 69–82 % | 69–82 % | 82–98 % |

Guts + Ice Punch is a dominated set: it gives up Rock Slide (2x into Rotom-Heat and Armarouge, spread) and gains no KO,
because unboosted Ice Punch misses the Mega-Z OHKO that Sheer Force gets on every roll. Mega-Z is in 10 of the 20
modelled sixes. The deficit shows in every segment (−12 even in games with no Trick Room), so it is not just the memo
bug. A 14-point gap between two abilities on the same moves is large, but a guaranteed-OHKO breakpoint on his most
common Mega can produce it. Per-six cells are n = 24 (±19), so the per-opponent swings (h07: 67 / 25 / 96 for nC19 /
xG_ip / xSF_ip) are mostly noise.

What it really shows: **ability-only changes moved our preview in 45–60 % of games, and dropping Mach Punch locked the
AI into one lead for all 480 games.** The heuristic preview is the dominant source of variance in set comparisons.
Discordant pairs run at 27–32 % even for a belief-only change, so paired n = 480 resolves about ±5 points, not less.

### 3.4 Other fidelity notes

- **Mean dice over-values inaccurate moves.** In search, every chance event resolves the likelier way, so Hydro Pump
  (80 %) always hits. Both game-32 replays show Scarf Pelipper locked into Hydro Pump rather than Weather Ball (100 %).
  The heuristic uses accuracy (`accOf`), the engine's clones don't. This matters most for Choice-locked attackers and
  for Jacky (Leaf Storm, Stone Edge, Draco, Eruption's partners).
- **HP bucketing.** The heuristic's damage cache computes every attacker above 50 % HP as if at 100 % (`bucket`,
  `ai/engine.mjs:38`). Fine for most moves, wrong for **Eruption** (Torkoal, Week 5): at 55 % HP it is computed at 150
  power instead of about 82.
- **Time per decision does not change results** (no time budget in the engine; `check-nC19` reproduced its 26 games).
  It only changes how many games fit in a day. So the 1.1 s → 14–20 s swing is a throughput problem: budget n up front
  and stop runs that can't resolve the question (§4).
- **Winner's curse in the shortlist.** nC19 scored 58.1 % on 160 games (seed 5200000) and 67.2 % on 480 fresh games: a
  9-point jump. The shortlist picks the luckiest of 8 at ±7.6; the fresh run is the right number to quote, and the
  first-round margins between shortlisted sixes should not be quoted as findings.
- **Persona coverage.** Personas exist for `tr` and `rock` leads only. The plan that actually beat us (fast Kleavor +
  Rotom-Heat offense, played with commitment by a human) has no persona.
- **Known open bug:** the engine keeps the believed set after a Mega reveal (round-1 §5.8). Still open.

5. **Re-baseline after every engine fix**: run the previous headline six and the new one on the same seeds. The
   difference is what the fix changed.
6. **Segment floor.** Before shipping, list every opposing segment worth ≥ 5 % of weight with n and CI. Any segment
   under 40 % gets a targeted set or lead test (one or two variants, paired) before the guide says "no fix".
7. **Separate team value from preview.** For set / ability comparisons, also report the same-pick subset and a run
   under one fixed preview table; when the preview moves in > 30 % of games, say the result is a preview effect until
   shown otherwise.
8. **Statistics budget.** Use paired n = 480 (±5) for set decisions, stop early when the interval excludes ±3, and
   don't run per-six cells at n = 24 as evidence (±19).
9. **Hand calcs include the whole turn:** recoil, contact punishment (Rocky Helmet, Rough Skin, both Parental Bond
   hits), Intimidate, Choice Scarf speed variants of the opposing mon, and weather order (§4.1).

## 6. Candidate method rules for playbook §3 (worded as rules)

10. **A human loss outranks the sim.** When a mock or real game is lost to a plan the sim rated ≥ 60 % for us, capture
    the replay, replay its key turns in the engine, and name the cause (team, AI blind spot, belief, or piloting) before
    rebuilding.
11. **After an engine fix, re-run the old headline six and the new one on the same seeds.** If the fix doesn't move the
    old six against the plan that beat it, the sim still can't see that failure.
12. **Segment floor:** report every opposing segment worth ≥ 5 % of weight with n and a CI. A segment under 40 % needs a
    targeted set or lead test before the guide may say "no fix".
13. **Beliefs are checked by eye:** print the closed-sheet belief table for both sides of every new pool; every species
    on both rosters must be in it, and no belief may come from an automatic item substitution.
14. **Generators never substitute items silently**; an item clash is an error, and each modelled six's items are chosen
    as a human would.
15. **Determinism is tested:** a game replayed alone must match its batch record, including Trick Room games. Any
    module-level cache must be keyed by set and side, never by slot.
16. **Preview effects are reported separately:** for any set or ability change, give the same-pick subset alongside the
    paired total; a change that moves the AI's preview in > 30 % of games is a preview result until shown otherwise.
17. **Judge one-shot surprises on games 2–3:** an ability or set whose value depends on the opponent not knowing it
    (Guts without Flame Orb) is scored with the opponent's belief updated, because the sheet is fixed for the Bo3.
18. **Hand calcs cover the whole exchange:** recoil, contact punishment (per hit under Parental Bond), Intimidate, the
    opposing mon's Scarf speed, and weather order on the lead.
19. **A "preview threat" value must survive a human-opponent check:** if a mon is brought < 10 % of games but removing
    it costs > 5 points, show which opposing brings changed and argue that a human would change them too.
